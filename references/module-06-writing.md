## MODULE 06 --- MASTER CASE STUDY WRITING INTELLIGENCE

### 1. Purpose

Transform the assignment, evidence, research and analysis into a clear,
coherent and credible case study adapted to its discipline, audience and
purpose.

### 2. Writing architecture selection

Use Module 06A's adaptive classification and framework-selection protocol before choosing a structure. Do not simply choose from a list of templates. Determine the case's deliverable, subject, purpose, dominant discipline, lifecycle stage, actual activities, evidence, audience and output format.

Construct a bespoke narrative architecture that follows the case's real logic and makes its central contribution clear. Use discipline-specific structures and artefacts; combine lenses only where they add distinct value. A product/UX portfolio case, for example, should explain how evidence became design decisions and interfaces, while an engineering implementation case should explain requirements, technical decisions, implementation and verification.

The patterns are adaptable, not mandatory templates. Do not default to a generic Problem–Solution–Results structure when another narrative better represents the work.

### 3. Structural component library

Use relevant components: - Title. - Executive summary or abstract. -
Introduction. - Background and context. - Case profile. - Problem or
opportunity. - Objectives and research questions. - Stakeholders. -
Literature or contextual review. - Methodology and methods. - Evidence
and findings. - Analysis and interpretation. - Decisions and
rationale. - Intervention or solution. - Implementation. - Outcomes and
evaluation. - Discussion. - Limitations. - Lessons learned. -
Recommendations. - Conclusion. - References. - Appendices.

Include only sections that serve the assignment.

### 4. Central argument and narrative

Establish: - What happened or is being examined. - Why it matters. -
What problem or opportunity existed. - What decisions or interventions
occurred. - What evidence shows. - What can reasonably be concluded. -
What lessons or implications follow.

A compelling narrative must not outrun the evidence.

### 5. Evidence-to-writing alignment

Every material factual or analytical claim must have an appropriate
basis. Preserve claim status and qualification during drafting.

Do not: - Invent missing stages to make a story complete. - Convert
assumptions into events. - Claim implementation where only a proposal
exists. - Attribute team actions to one person without support. - Turn a
reported result into an independently verified result.

### 6. Analytical depth

Move beyond description when the assignment requires it. Apply
appropriate levels: - Description: What happened? - Interpretation: What
does it mean? - Explanation: Why might it have happened? - Analysis:
What relationships, mechanisms or patterns are present? - Evaluation:
How well did it work against defined criteria? - Critical examination:
What assumptions, alternatives and limitations matter? - Implications:
What follows for practice, policy, theory or future decisions?

### 7. Discipline-specific adaptation

Use the language, conventions, evidence expectations and analytical
standards of the relevant discipline. Explain specialist terms where the
audience requires it.

### 8. Personal and organisational contribution

Distinguish: - User's own contribution. - Team contribution. -
Organisational contribution. - External contribution. - Independently
verified contribution. - Reported but unverified contribution.

Do not exaggerate ownership or authorship.

### 9. Failure and inconclusive outcomes

Treat failure, partial success, negative findings and inconclusive
results as legitimate case outcomes. Analyse causes and lessons without
forcing a success narrative.

### 10. Quantitative and causal claims

Check definitions, denominators, baselines, timeframes, measurement
methods and comparability. Distinguish observed change, association,
contribution and demonstrated causation.

### 11. Audience, tone and length

Adapt formality, terminology, detail, structure and length to the
audience and assignment. Maintain clarity and precision. Remove filler,
repetition and generic statements.

### 12. Editorial process

Draft, review, revise and proofread for: - Accuracy. - Coherence. -
Logical flow. - Analytical depth. - Evidence alignment. - Appropriate
tone. - Consistency. - Grammar and style. - Citation integrity. -
Assignment compliance.
