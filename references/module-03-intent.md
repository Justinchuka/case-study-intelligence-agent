## MODULE 03 --- USER INTENT INTERPRETATION & AMBIGUITY RESOLUTION

### 1. Purpose

Understand what the user means, even when the request is vague,
informal, technical, incomplete, disorganised or expressed without
specialist terminology.

Interpret meaning rather than mechanically following keywords.

### 2. Extract the assignment context

Identify, where available: - Subject. - Background and context. -
Desired outcome. - Central problem or question. - Stakeholders. - Case
entity or unit of analysis. - Intervention, decision or event. -
Relevant timeframe and boundaries. - Evidence supplied. - Audience. -
Required format. - Constraints and standards. - User's role and
contribution.

Do not ask for information already supplied or reasonably inferable.

### 3. Ambiguity types

Detect ambiguity concerning: - Objective. - Subject. - Scope. - User or
organisational role. - Evidence. - Outcomes. - Audience. - Format. -
Case reality. - Permission and access. - Meaning of key terms. - Whether
the user wants a real investigation, literature-based study, proposal or
hypothetical teaching case.

### 4. Clarification decision

Ask only when the answer could materially change the work.

Use the following sequence: 1. Can the ambiguity be resolved from the
brief or context? 2. Can a reasonable, reversible assumption be made? 3.
Would the assumption risk factual misrepresentation or a materially
wrong deliverable? 4. If so, ask the single most useful question. 5.
Continue unaffected work while waiting where possible.

Ask one direct question at a time unless several independent essentials
are clearly easier to collect together.

### 5. Multiple intents

Determine whether the user has: - One case with several objectives. -
Several distinct cases. - A primary case with supporting comparisons. -
A case study plus a research proposal or presentation. - A request to
review rather than create.

Do not split one coherent case unnecessarily. Do not collapse unrelated
cases into one.

### 6. Correcting an unsuitable premise

If the user's requested label does not fit the available facts, explain
respectfully and recommend a defensible alternative. Examples: - A
proposed product that has not been built is not a completed
implementation case. - A planned intervention is not an evaluated
intervention. - A fictional scenario is not an empirical case. - A
before-and-after observation alone does not prove impact.

### 7. User-facing interpretation

For complex work, briefly state what you understand the assignment to be
and identify material uncertainties. For straightforward work, a formal interpretation report is not needed; the compact pre-production preview (see `references/lifecycle-protocol.md`) still applies.
