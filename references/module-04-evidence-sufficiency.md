## MODULE 04 --- INFORMATION SUFFICIENCY, EVIDENCE ASSESSMENT & RESEARCH REQUIREMENTS

### 1. Purpose

Determine whether the available information is sufficient to produce the
requested case study responsibly, what is missing, what can be
researched and what limitations must remain visible.

### 2. Evidence dimensions

Assess relevant evidence concerning: 1. Context and background. 2.
Problem or opportunity. 3. Stakeholders and affected groups. 4.
Investigation and diagnosis. 5. Decisions and rationale. 6. Intervention
or response. 7. Implementation and execution. 8. Validation and
measurement. 9. Outcomes and impact. 10. Limitations and lessons.

Not every case requires all ten dimensions.

### 3. Evidence categories

Recognise: - Primary evidence. - Secondary evidence. - Documentary
evidence. - Quantitative evidence. - Qualitative evidence. - Expert
testimony. - Anecdotal evidence. - Observational evidence. -
Administrative or operational records. - Hypothetical or simulated
evidence.

### 4. Evidence quality

Assess: - Authenticity. - Relevance. - Reliability. - Specificity. -
Recency. - Consistency. - Traceability. - Methodological quality. -
Representativeness. - Independence and potential bias.

A source may be useful for one claim but insufficient for another.

### 5. Claim status

Assign appropriate status: - **Verified fact:** Supported by adequate
verification. - **Reported information:** Supplied or asserted by a
person or organisation but not independently verified. - **Sourced
claim:** Supported by an identifiable external source. - **Inference:**
Reasoned interpretation from available information. - **Assumption:** A
working proposition adopted because information is missing. -
**Hypothesis:** A proposition to be investigated. - **Projection:** A
forward-looking estimate. - **Simulated:** Generated or constructed for
a model or hypothetical case. - **Unknown:** Not established.

Never silently upgrade a claim's status.

### 6. Sufficiency assessment

Classify the assignment as: - **Sufficient:** Enough information exists
for the requested work. - **Partially sufficient:** Work can proceed,
but some claims, sections or conclusions need qualification. -
**Insufficient:** A critical gap prevents responsible completion of a
material requirement.

Assess sufficiency at section and claim level, not only for the document
as a whole.

An internal 0--3 assessment may be used for relevant evidence
dimensions: - 0: absent. - 1: weak or indirect. - 2: adequate with
limitations. - 3: strong and well-supported.

A critical missing item overrides an aggregate score.

### 7. Gap prioritisation

Classify gaps as: - **Critical:** Prevents a defensible conclusion or
essential deliverable. - **Important:** Materially reduces confidence or
depth. - **Optional:** Would improve completeness but is not necessary.

Request targeted evidence. Do not demand every possible document.

### 8. Research requirement decision

Determine whether external research is: - Necessary. - Useful but
optional. - Unnecessary. - Prohibited by user instruction.

External research cannot substitute for private evidence about an
organisation's actual internal activities or results. Public sources can
establish context, standards, market conditions and published findings,
but cannot verify confidential events without evidence.

### 9. Execution routes

**Route A --- Direct execution:** Evidence is sufficient and the user
has authorised the requested work.

**Route B --- Research-led execution:** Research can responsibly fill
contextual or knowledge gaps.

**Route C --- Evidence request:** A critical private or case-specific
gap requires user material.

**Route D --- Qualified or alternative case:** Adapt scope, present a
proposal, use a literature-based approach or construct an explicitly
hypothetical case when appropriate.

Never abandon a viable assignment merely because some evidence is
unavailable. Narrow claims and disclose limitations.
